## 0. Executive Summary

ROMAS Wire is not a newsletter. It is the **media + intelligence + audience operating system** for the ROMAS ecosystem, and the front door through which radiation oncology professionals will enter the ROMAS web app at launch in five weeks.

**The thesis in one sentence:**
Own the trusted intelligence layer of radiation oncology *before* selling software into it — because in a clinically conservative, AI-disrupted, tightly-networked specialty, **attention is the moat, and trust compounds faster than features**.

**Why this works now:**

- Radiation oncology has **no dominant intelligence brand** comparable to Imaging Wire in radiology. The category is open.
- The field is small enough to dominate (~5,500 US radiation oncologists, ~8,000 medical physicists, ~20,000 therapists/dosimetrists) and high-value enough to monetize.
- AI disruption is accelerating faster than clinicians can track it. ASTRO and the joint ESTRO-AAPM statements frame AI in RT as requiring formal validation, commissioning, QA, and monitoring — meaning **interpretation, not aggregation**, is the unmet need.
- ROMAS already has the four assets that kill 90% of media startups in their crib: **distribution (email lists), domain expertise, AI capability, and a software product to monetize the audience into**.

**The play:**
Launch a free, daily, AI-assisted but human-approved intelligence brief in 5 weeks, branded as **ROMAS Wire** (flagship) under the **ROMAS Intelligence** umbrella. Use it as the warm-up campaign for the ROMAS web app launch, then evolve it into a Bloomberg-Terminal-class structured intelligence graph for radiation oncology.

## 3. Product Architecture

### 3.1 The daily ROMAS Wire brief

**Target length:** 5-minute read. **Cadence:** Weekday mornings, 6:30 AM ET delivery.

| Slot | Format | Word count | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero Story | 1 item · headline + 4-question frame | 200–300 | Literature, FDA, Guideline, or Vendor agent |
| Top AI in RadOnc | 2–3 items | 80–120 each | AI Startup + Vendor agents |
| Paper of the Day | 1 item · journal, design, findings, limits, "what it means" | 200 | Literature agent |
| Industry Moves | 3–5 bullets | 30–50 each | Vendor + AI Startup agents |
| Quick Hits | 5 headlines | 20 each | All agents |
| **ROMAS Insight** | 1 short take | 80–120 | Editorial (you / clinician advisor) |
| Podcast embed | 5–10 min audio | — | Generated from same content |

**Rules:**
- One hero, never two. Hierarchy enforces habit.
- ROMAS Insight is signed (initials or role) and explicitly labeled as opinion.
- Every claim links to a primary source (DOI, PMID, FDA record, CMS document, vendor press URL).

### 3.2 The weekly ROMAS Weekly

**Cadence:** Sunday evening 7 PM ET (lands in inbox before Monday clinic).

Content:
- **Top 5 Papers of the Week** with structured critique (PICO summary, design, key findings, limitations, applicability score).
- **Practice Delta** — what changed in guidelines or reimbursement that should alter clinic Monday.
- **Conference watch** during ASTRO / AAPM / ESTRO / ASCO.
- **ROMAS Long Take** — one signed essay, 600–900 words.

### 3.3 Podcast network (phased)

| Show | Length | Cadence | Phase |

**Hard constraint at launch:** The Q&A agent answers **only from ROMAS-approved content and a whitelist of primary sources**. No open-web freeform retrieval. This protects against hallucination liability in a clinical context.

## 4. The Intelligence Engine (Backend)

### 4.1 Architectural philosophy

**ROMAS owns the brain. The orchestration framework is plumbing.**

Do not let LangGraph, CrewAI, OpenCore, AutoGen, or any other framework become the brain of ROMAS. They are substrate. ROMAS owns:

- The clinical content schema
- The radiation oncology ontology (disease sites × modalities × techniques × audience × evidence levels)
- The scoring rubric
- The source verification rules
- The editorial workflow
- The compliance and audit trail
- The subscriber graph

### 4.2 The three-speed stack

| Layer | Technology | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Layer 1: Ingestion** | Deterministic Python workers + cron + RSS | NO AI here. Pull raw data only. |
| **Layer 2: Normalization** | Structured extraction (LLM-assisted, schema-validated) | Convert raw → canonical Content Object |
| **Layer 3: Orchestration** | **LangGraph** + Claude Sonnet 4.6 / GPT-5 | Score, draft, route, gate |
| **Layer 4: Human Approval** | Custom editorial dashboard (Next.js + Supabase) | Required for all clinical content at launch |
| **Layer 5: Distribution** | Beehiiv (or Kit) + Transistor + Twilio + custom web | Fan out to email, web, audio, SMS, social |

**Why LangGraph over autonomous agent frameworks:** healthcare intelligence cannot tolerate infinite loops, unstable planning, or unobservable decision paths. LangGraph gives you branching workflows, retries, human-in-the-loop checkpoints, deterministic state, and audit-grade logs. AutoGen and pure ReAct loops do not.

### 4.3 The canonical Content Object

Every item in the system — whether it ever publishes or not — is stored as:

```json
{
  "id": "uuid",
  "source_url": "https://...",
  "source_type": "pubmed | fda | cms | astro | vendor | arxiv | trial | press",
  "source_organization": "ASTRO",
  "identifiers": {"pmid": "...", "doi": "...", "510k": "...", "nct": "..."},
  "publication_time": "2026-05-12T08:00:00Z",
  "embargo_until": null,
  "raw_text_hash": "sha256:...",
  "extracted_claims": [{"claim": "...", "evidence_span": "...", "confidence": 0.91}],
  "tags": {
    "modality": ["Adaptive RT", "SBRT"],
    "disease_site": ["Prostate"],
    "audience": ["Physicist", "Physician"],
    "technique": ["MR-Linac"]
  },
  "scores": {
    "clinical_relevance": 0.91,
    "ai_disruption": 0.74,
    "physics_relevance": 0.62,
    "novelty": 0.83,
    "operational_impact": 0.55,
    "confidence": 0.88
  },
  "evidence_level": "Phase III RCT",
  "editorial_state": "draft | in_review | approved | published | rejected",
  "drafts": {"brief": "...", "weekly": "...", "podcast_script": "...", "social": "..."},
  "romas_insight": "...",
  "approved_by": "user_id",
  "approved_at": "ts"
}
```

**This object is the moat.** Over 12 months it becomes the structured intelligence graph of radiation oncology — a dataset no competitor can recreate quickly.

### 4.4 The seven agents

| # | Agent | Primary sources | Key constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Literature** | PubMed E-utilities, Crossref, arXiv, Semantic Scholar | Never publish from title alone; require abstract + (ideally) full-text |
| 2 | **FDA** | openFDA + official FDA AI/ML device list + 510(k) database | openFDA is *discovery only* — verify against official record before publishing |
| 3 | **Guideline** | ASTRO, AAPM TGs, ESTRO, NCCN, ACR Appropriateness | Classify change type: new / revised / endorsed / retired / superseded → emit Practice Delta |
| 4 | **Reimbursement** | CMS PFS, MCD (NCDs/LCDs, weekly LCD downloads), MLN Connects, Federal Register API | Federal Register API is informational — verify high-stakes claims against the official PDF/govinfo |
| 5 | **Vendor Intelligence** | Elekta, Varian, RaySearch, Radformation, MIM, Siemens, GE newsrooms + investor channels | Cross-link every vendor event to FDA / CMS / trial / abstract signals |
| 6 | **Conference** | ASTRO, AAPM, ESTRO, ASCO abstract portals | **Embargo-aware object model with hard publish lock** — ASTRO and ASCO embargoes are non-negotiable |
| 7 | **AI Startup Tracker** | SEC EDGAR, NIH RePORTER, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA, company sites, hiring signals; Crunchbase as optional enrichment | Multi-source corroboration before "funded $X" claims |

### 4.5 The ROMAS Scoring Engine

Every Content Object is scored 0–100 on six axes:

1. **Clinical relevance** — will this change patient management?
2. **AI disruption potential** — does this signal automation or augmentation?
3. **Physics relevance** — commissioning, QA, planning, dose implications?
4. **Operational impact** — staffing, workflow, throughput?
5. **Novelty** — incremental vs. paradigm-shifting?
6. **Confidence** — source quality × corroboration × evidence level

A composite **Signal Score** drives the publish queue. Items below threshold are stored but not surfaced. Editors can override scores; overrides train the rubric.

## 5. Editorial Standards & Trust Architecture

**This product dies the day a clinician catches a hallucination.**

### 5.1 The five inviolable rules

1. **Every public item cites a primary source by URL.** No "according to recent studies."
2. **Every interpretation is labeled** as ROMAS Insight, ROMAS Take, or Opinion — never blended into factual prose.
3. **Every clinical claim has a claim-level evidence trail** in the internal workspace. An editor must be able to click the claim and see the source span the model used.
4. **No publish without human approval** for Literature, Reimbursement, FDA, and Guideline content at launch. Vendor roundups and conference logistics may move to spot-checked auto-publish after 60 days of clean correction rates (<1%).
5. **Embargoes are sacred.** Conference content has a hard publish lock until embargo lifts. Violations get ASTRO/ASCO authors disinvited from giving us interviews — and we need them.

### 5.2 Three-state approval system

```
DISCOVERY (autonomous) → DRAFTING (assisted) → RELEASE (human)
                                                    │
                                                    ├── Approve → Publish
                                                    ├── Edit → Republish queue
                                                    └── Reject → Logged + scored
```

Track correction rate per agent. After 60 days, agents with <1% correction rate on low-risk content categories graduate to auto-publish with daily spot-check.

### 5.3 Conflict-of-interest disclosure

Because ROMAS sells software into this audience, every issue carries a one-line standing disclosure: *"ROMAS Wire is published by ROMAS, which builds radiation oncology software. Sponsored items are clearly labeled. Editorial coverage is independent."*

## 6. Compliance & Deliverability (Do Not Skip)

### 6.1 Email regulatory stack

| Requirement | Rule | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **CAN-SPAM (US)** | Truthful headers, physical address, working unsubscribe, honored within 10 business days | Bake into Beehiiv footer + monitor unsubscribe queue daily |
| **Google Sender Rules (≥5,000 msgs/day to Gmail)** | SPF or DKIM aligned with From domain; one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058); spam rate <0.10% (avoid 0.30%+) | Configure SPF + DKIM via Beehiiv CNAMEs; set up DMARC on sending subdomain; gradual volume ramp |
| **GDPR / UK GDPR** | Explicit consent, recordable, revocable | Use double opt-in for any EU/UK contacts; soft re-permission for any imported list |
| **HIPAA** | Not directly triggered (no PHI handled), but document the boundary | Add "no PHI" clause to ToS and subscriber agreement |

### 6.2 The list-warmup protocol (CRITICAL)

You have valuable existing email lists. **Blasting them on day one will destroy domain reputation.** The 14-day warmup:

| Day | Action | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Warmed-domain seed send to 100 friendly contacts | 100 |
| 3–5 | Soft re-permission invitation to top-engagement segment of existing lists | 500/day |
| 6–8 | Ramp to engaged opt-ins only | 1,500/day |
| 9–11 | Add second engagement tier | 3,500/day |
| 12–14 | Open to full re-permissioned list | 7,000+/day |

Monitor at every step: open rate (target >35% for niche professional), bounce rate (<2%), spam complaint rate (<0.10% absolute floor), unsubscribe rate (<0.5%).

### 6.3 Domain strategy

- Primary brand domain: `romaswire.com` (acquire week 1) OR subdomain `wire.romas.health`.
- Sending domain: `mail.romaswire.com` (warm separately from root).
- Authentication: SPF + DKIM + DMARC (p=quarantine → p=reject by month 3).
