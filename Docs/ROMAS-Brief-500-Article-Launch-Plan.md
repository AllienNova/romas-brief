## 0. The Strategic Premise

The 500 articles are not filler. They are a **credibility scaffold**. When a radiation oncologist or medical physicist lands on ROMAS Wire on launch day, every category they care about must look populated, recent, sourced, and authoritative. The distribution below is engineered so that:

- **No category looks empty.** Every audience segment (physicist, physician, dosimetrist, therapist, resident, industry) finds ≥30 relevant articles.
- **No region looks ignored.** US, EU, UK, APAC, and Global all carry meaningful share.
- **No content type is missing.** Hero stories, paper critiques, FDA/CE-mark briefs, guideline practice deltas, reimbursement explainers, vendor moves, conference recaps, primers, and explainers are all represented.
- **Freshness signal is strong.** ~60% of articles are dated within the last 90 days; ~30% within 91–365 days; ~10% are evergreen primers (clearly labeled).
- **Trust signal is strong.** Every article has a primary-source URL, an audience tag, a region tag, and a Signal Score.

## 5. Editorial Constraints That Stay Inviolable

Even during the launch ramp, the five rules from the runbook hold:

1. **Every article cites a primary-source URL inline.** No "recent studies show."
2. **Every interpretation is labeled** as ROMAS Insight / ROMAS Take / Opinion.
3. **Every clinical claim has a claim-level evidence trail** in the editorial workspace.
4. **No publish without human approval** for Literature, Reimbursement, FDA, and Guideline content during launch.
5. **Embargoes are sacred.** Backfill content sourced from conference abstracts must be post-embargo (so ASTRO/ESTRO/AAPM 2025 recaps are clean; 2026 conference content respects embargoes).

**Padding-prevention rule:** If a category's allocation cannot be filled with items at Signal Score ≥55 by launch, ship that category short rather than weaken it. Reader trust > category symmetry.

### Sample 1 — AI Auto-Contouring (Hero candidate, Signal 88)

**Category:** AI in Radiation Oncology → Auto-contouring
**Audience:** Physician (primary), Physicist, Dosimetrist
**Region:** Global (single-institution; methodology generalizable)
**Signal Scores:** Clinical 90 · AI 95 · Physics 75 · Operational 80 · Novelty 85 · Confidence 88 · **Composite 88**

**Headline (≤90 chars):** AI-Assisted Prostate Contouring Cuts Time 41% — But Automation Bias Is Real

**What happened.** A single-institution validation study published in the *Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics* (January 2026 collection) tested Limbus AI's contouring software against manual contouring by two radiation oncologists with >10 years of experience, using a three-expert consensus panel as the reference standard ([JACMP via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12715409/)). Patients had two contouring rounds — one fully manual and one AI-assisted with oncologist review — separated by two months to control for memory bias.

**Why it matters.** AI-assisted contouring reduced time substantially while preserving target/OAR accuracy on standard dosimetric metrics. The critical finding: even experienced oncologists *under-corrected* AI contours relative to the consensus standard — automation bias measurably degraded the final contour when reviewers anchored on the AI starting point. The authors call for standardized QA protocols specifically designed to detect automation bias in AI-assisted workflows.

**ROMAS Insight (interpretation).** Time savings are real and the productivity case for AI contouring is settled in prostate. The unsettled question is *quality assurance design*: every clinic deploying AI contouring needs a peer-review or blinded-comparison protocol that surfaces automation bias before it reaches the patient. Centers should not assume vendor-supplied validation suffices. **— ROMAS Insight**

### Sample 2 — Long-Term MR-Linac Patient Experience (Strong, Signal 81)

**Category:** Clinical RT → Adaptive RT / MR-Linac
**Audience:** Physician, Dosimetrist, Therapist
**Region:** Europe (Germany)
**Signal Scores:** Clinical 85 · AI 45 · Physics 70 · Operational 88 · Novelty 75 · Confidence 90 · **Composite 81**

**Headline:** Heidelberg's 231-Patient MR-Linac Study Quantifies the Adaptive Workflow Burden

**What happened.** A subgroup analysis from Heidelberg University Hospital's prospective MRIdian Linac registry — 231 patients, 286 lesions, median 35-month follow-up — reports patient-reported outcomes and staff-rated workflow complexity for online MR-guided radiotherapy with and without daily plan adaptation, published in *Frontiers in Oncology* on 3 February 2026 ([Frontiers in Oncology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2026.1700649/full)). The study covered January 2019 to May 2021, with online plan adaptation implemented in February 2020.

**Why it matters.** This is one of the longest-duration real-world MR-Linac datasets to-date and the first to systematically capture *staff complexity scores* alongside patient experience. Adaptive workflows scored higher on staff-rated complexity but did not degrade patient-reported tolerability over multi-year follow-up. For programs evaluating MR-Linac adoption or expanding adaptive indications, this answers the durability question that early-adopter case series could not.

**ROMAS Insight.** Adaptive MR-Linac is now a multi-year mature workflow — the question for community programs is no longer "does it work" but "can our staffing model absorb the complexity load." Centers should benchmark their own staff-complexity scores against this Heidelberg baseline before scaling indications. **— ROMAS Insight**

Brazil and Thailand adoption is the leading indicator to watch.

**ROMAS Insight.** The international clearance pace is now the single best leading indicator for which AI-RT vendors will hit $50M+ ARR within 24 months. Vendors who clear only FDA are leaving 60% of the addressable market on the table; vendors stacking ANVISA + TGA + Health Canada + Thailand FDA + EU CE-mark are signaling commercial seriousness. **— ROMAS Insight**

### Sample 4 — Reimbursement Practice Delta (Strong, Signal 83)

**Category:** Reimbursement & Policy → US CMS
**Audience:** Physician, Industry, Operations
**Region:** US
**Signal Scores:** Clinical 65 · AI 30 · Physics 50 · Operational 95 · Novelty 80 · Confidence 90 · **Composite 83**

**Headline:** 2026 MPFS Collapses RT Coding into Three Levels — Mapping Errors Will Hit Q1 Revenue

**What happened.** The 2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule restructures radiation oncology coding by collapsing treatment-specific codes — including IMRT and electron beam therapy — into three broader levels, accompanied by a ~2.5% efficiency-adjustment reduction in wRVUs affecting both professional and technical reimbursement ([VMG Health analysis](https://vmghealth.com/insights/blog/2026-medicare-fee-schedule-changes-in-radiation-oncology/)). Discontinued codes require active remapping; revenue projections built on 2025 historicals will overstate 2026 expectations.

**Why it matters.** This is not a minor tweak. Practices that don't remap discontinued codes will see denied claims, not just lower per-service reimbursement. Coding and billing teams need engagement *now* — Q1 2026 claims are already in flight. High-volume IMRT programs are most exposed because their case mix concentrated in codes that were collapsed.

**ROMAS Insight.** Every radiation oncology practice administrator should run a parallel 2025-vs-2026 revenue model this month, not in March. The combination of code collapse + 2.5% efficiency adjustment is a 5–8% top-line revenue swing for typical IMRT-heavy programs. Practices on the RO Model should additionally model the interaction effect. **— ROMAS Insight**

### Sample 5 — EU AI Act Practice Delta (Hero candidate, Signal 86)

**Category:** Regulatory → EU
**Audience:** Industry, Physician, Physicist
**Region:** Europe
**Signal Scores:** Clinical 70 · AI 95 · Physics 75 · Operational 90 · Novelty 90 · Confidence 85 · **Composite 86**

**Headline:** EU AI Act Omnibus Deal (May 7) Streamlines — But Doesn't Soften — Medical AI Rules

**What happened.** The EU Council and European Parliament reached provisional agreement on a Digital Omnibus amendment to the EU AI Act on May 7, 2026, simplifying procedural requirements for AI-based medical devices but preserving their automatic "high-risk" classification ([European Commission — Regulatory Framework for AI](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai); **fact-check pre-publish**: regulatory-analyst to substitute the specific Council press-release URL for the May 7 provisional agreement once located via `consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/`). Notified Bodies conducting MDR/IVDR assessments can now simultaneously evaluate AI Act compliance, and technical documentation/post-market monitoring reports (PMS, PSURs, PMCF) can be integrated rather than duplicated. Devices placed on market before the cut-off date with unchanged design can remain without new assessment; any significant design modification triggers full AI Act compliance.

**Why it matters.** For RT-AI vendors active in the EU, this changes commercialization economics meaningfully. The single conformity assessment path collapses two regulatory workstreams into one, and integrated documentation reduces duplicative compliance overhead. The legacy-device carve-out protects current MR-Linac AI modules, RaySearch deep-learning components, and Brainlab features that shipped pre-cutoff — but the "significant design modification" trigger means version bumps must be carefully scoped.

**ROMAS Insight.** This is procedural simplification, not regulatory softening — and that's actually the right outcome for clinical trust. Vendors planning major AI feature releases in 2026 should re-baseline their EU launch timelines now; the integrated conformity path can compress 6–9 months off prior estimates if executed cleanly. **— ROMAS Insight**
